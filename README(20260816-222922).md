# Goal-Based Portfolio Optimisation

A quantitative portfolio optimisation framework that evaluates how **portfolio composition and the timing of financial goals** affect the probability of achieving a long-term wealth target.

The project uses historical market data for five NSE-listed stocks, discrete portfolio enumeration, Monte Carlo simulation, and continuous optimisation to identify portfolios that maximise the probability of reaching a target terminal wealth under different withdrawal schedules.

## Key Results

The framework evaluates two retirement/goal sequences over a **20-year horizon** with a terminal target of **₹1.5 Crore**.

| Metric | Sequence A | Sequence B |
|---|---:|---:|
| Goal structure | Aggressive early withdrawals | Backloaded withdrawals |
| Optimal portfolio | 100% ITC | 75% ITC + 25% HDFC Bank |
| Probability of success | **0.04%** | **89.7%** |
| Borrowing required | From Year 3 | None |

### Main finding

The analysis shows that **goal timing can dominate stock selection**. The backloaded plan gives the portfolio more time to compound before withdrawals, while the early-withdrawal plan triggers borrowing at 12% p.a., creating a rapidly growing debt burden.

## Methodology

### 1. Market Data

Daily adjusted closing prices are obtained for the five stocks.

From daily returns, the framework estimates:

- Annualised expected return
- Annualised volatility
- Annualised covariance matrix

Annualisation uses **252 trading days**.

### 2. Goal Definition

The framework models:

- **20-year investment horizon**
- Initial monthly savings of **₹20,000**
- Savings growth of **4% annually**
- Borrowing rate of **12% p.a.**
- Terminal wealth target of **₹1.5 Crore**

Two different goal sequences are tested.

### Sequence A — Aggressive Early Goals

```text
Year 3   → ₹15 Lakh
Year 7   → ₹25 Lakh
Year 12  → ₹30 Lakh
Year 20  → ₹1.5 Crore
```

### Sequence B — Backloaded Goals

```text
Year 8   → ₹10 Lakh
Year 12  → ₹20 Lakh
Year 16  → ₹40 Lakh
Year 20  → ₹1.5 Crore
```

## Portfolio Search

The optimisation uses discrete portfolio weights:

```text
Weight values = {0%, 25%, 50%, 75%, 100%}
```

subject to:

```text
Σ wi = 1
```

This produces **70 valid portfolios** across the five stocks.

Each portfolio is evaluated using Monte Carlo simulation.

---

## Monte Carlo Simulation

For every candidate portfolio, the framework:

1. Calculates portfolio expected return and variance.
2. Generates **5,000 stochastic paths** over the 20-year horizon.
3. Adds annual savings to portfolio wealth.
4. Applies scheduled goal withdrawals.
5. Introduces borrowing when portfolio wealth is insufficient to meet a goal.
6. Compounds outstanding debt at **12% p.a.**
7. Computes whether terminal net wealth reaches **₹1.5 Crore**.

The optimisation objective is:

```text
Maximise P(Net Wealth at Year 20 ≥ ₹1.5 Crore)
```

---

## Continuous Optimisation

As an additional analysis, the project uses **SciPy SLSQP** to perform continuous portfolio optimisation.

The continuous formulation:

- Constrains portfolio weights to sum to 1
- Allows weights between -50% and 150%
- Maximises simulated probability of achieving the terminal goal
- Uses a separate Monte Carlo evaluation with 2,000 paths

This provides a comparison between the discrete portfolio search and continuous optimisation.

## Example Interpretation

The analysis demonstrates a key principle of goal-based investing:

> **A portfolio with strong expected returns cannot necessarily compensate for poorly timed withdrawals.**

For Sequence A, aggressive early withdrawals force borrowing from Year 3. At a **12% borrowing rate**, the resulting debt compounds significantly and makes the terminal target extremely difficult to achieve.

Sequence B delays the major withdrawals, allowing the portfolio and savings to compound for longer. The optimal allocation of **75% ITC and 25% HDFC Bank** achieves a reported **89.7% probability of reaching ₹1.5 Crore** under the simulation assumptions.

---

## Limitations

This is an academic quantitative modelling project and should not be interpreted as investment advice.

Important modelling assumptions include:

- Returns are represented using a stochastic simulation framework.
- Portfolio risk is estimated from historical return statistics.
- Goal withdrawals occur at predefined years.
- Borrowing is modelled at a constant 12% annual rate.
- Portfolio weights are restricted to predefined discrete values for the main optimisation.
- Results are sensitive to return, volatility, correlation, savings, borrowing, and goal assumptions.
- Monte Carlo probabilities are simulation estimates rather than guarantees.
